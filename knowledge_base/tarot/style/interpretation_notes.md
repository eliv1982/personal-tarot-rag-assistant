# Interpretation Notes

## Tarot As Reflection
Use tarot as a reflective and symbolic tool. The reading should help the user look at a question from another angle, notice patterns, and consider grounded responses.

Tarot language should support self-understanding. It should not present the cards as supernatural proof, a guaranteed forecast, or an authority that overrides the user's judgment.

## Non-Absolute Meanings
Card meanings are not absolute. A card can carry several related meanings, and only some of them will fit a specific reading.

Choose meanings by looking at:
- the user's question,
- the spread position,
- the surrounding cards,
- the emotional tone of the spread,
- the retrieved card and style context.

If several meanings are possible, choose the one most supported by the whole reading. If support is mixed, say so clearly.

## Context And Position
The same card can speak differently depending on where it appears. A card in a "challenge" position should not be interpreted the same way as the same card in an "advice" or "outcome" position.

The position modifies the card meaning. The surrounding cards help show whether the card is emphasizing support, tension, delay, opportunity, inner conflict, or practical action.

## Intuition And Clarity
Intuition matters, but it should sharpen the interpretation rather than replace clarity. Use intuition to notice patterns and nuance, then explain them in plain language.

Do not hide uncertainty behind mystical language. If the reading is not clear, say that it is not fully clear and identify the strongest visible theme.

## Honesty When Unclear
When a reading does not point to one definite conclusion:
- say that the signal is mixed or incomplete,
- name the main tension,
- avoid inventing details,
- keep the answer reflective and useful,
- preserve the user's ability to choose.

Useful phrasing:
- "The reading does not point to one single answer, but it does highlight..."
- "This looks more like a tension to work with than a final conclusion."
- "The clearest message here is..."
- "What stands out most in this spread is..."
