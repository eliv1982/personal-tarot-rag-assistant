# Card Interpretation Method

## Method
Interpret a card through the whole reading, not as an isolated dictionary entry.

Use this order:
1. Start with the user's question.
2. Identify the spread position and what that position asks the card to describe.
3. Read the card's core symbolic meaning.
4. Adjust the meaning through neighboring cards and the overall pattern.
5. Explain the result in simple, grounded language.

## Working With Card Meanings
Card definitions are prompts, not final answers. They point toward possible meanings that need to be tested against the reading context.

When a card has several possible meanings:
- prefer the meaning that best fits the question and position,
- mention ambiguity if two meanings both matter,
- avoid overloading the user with every possible definition,
- do not force a dramatic interpretation when a practical one fits better.

## Working With Positions
A position changes the function of a card:
- In a situation position, describe the current pattern or atmosphere.
- In an obstacle position, describe friction, resistance, or what is hard to integrate.
- In an advice position, describe a useful attitude, question, or next step.
- In an outcome position, describe a possible direction, not a fixed fate.
- In a self or inner-state position, focus on the user's perspective, feelings, or choices.

## Cross-Card Synthesis
After interpreting individual cards, synthesize the spread:
- Look for repeated suits, themes, numbers, or emotional tones.
- Notice whether cards support one another or point in different directions.
- If the reading contains a contradiction, name it plainly.
- Let the final interpretation reflect the whole spread, not only the most striking card.

## Reversed Cards
Treat reversed cards as nuance, not automatic negativity.

A reversal may suggest:
- blocked or delayed expression,
- internalized energy,
- resistance,
- excess or imbalance,
- a need to reconsider the card's usual expression,
- a theme that is present but harder to access.

Interpret reversals in relation to the question, position, and surrounding cards.

## User Agency
Do not make the cards sound like commands. A reading can suggest a direction, but the user chooses how to respond.

For possible outcomes, use language such as:
- "This may develop toward..."
- "The current pattern points toward..."
- "If nothing changes, this could suggest..."
- "A more constructive response may be..."
