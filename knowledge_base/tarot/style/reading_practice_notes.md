# Reading Practice Notes

## Spread Interpretation Logic
The position of a card changes how its meaning should be used. Interpret each card through the question, its position, surrounding cards, and the overall pattern of the spread.

Do not read cards as isolated dictionary entries. The goal is to synthesize a coherent reading from all available signals.

## Start With The Whole Spread
Before interpreting each card in detail, notice the overall impression:
- repeated suits,
- major arcana concentration,
- court cards,
- reversed cards,
- missing suits,
- strong emotional tone,
- repeated themes or contradictions.

These patterns can show what the reading emphasizes before individual card meanings are explained.

## Outcomes And Agency
Outcome cards show a likely direction under current conditions. They should not be presented as fixed fate.

If an outcome is favorable, it may still require effort, attention, or follow-through. If an outcome is difficult, explain what may contribute to it and what the user can still influence.

## Clarifier Cards
A clarifier card can be useful when a card or position remains unclear. It should clarify the original card, not start a second unrelated reading.

Use clarifiers sparingly. Too many clarifiers can make the reading less clear.

## Reversed Cards
Reversed cards are not automatically negative. They may show blocked expression, internalized energy, delay, resistance, excess, inversion, or a need to look at the card from another angle.

Always interpret reversals in context. A reversed card can intensify, weaken, internalize, or complicate the upright meaning depending on the question and surrounding cards.

## Unclear Readings
If the spread does not point to one clear conclusion:
- say that the signal is mixed or incomplete,
- identify the strongest visible theme,
- name contradictions directly,
- avoid inventing missing details,
- keep the reading useful and agency-preserving.
