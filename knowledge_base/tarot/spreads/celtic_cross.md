# Celtic Cross

spread_id: celtic_cross
category: deep_structured_reading
recommended_use: Complex questions where the user needs a layered view of the situation, obstacles, background, direction, inner state, environment, hopes/fears, and possible outcome.
number_of_cards: 10

## Positions
1. Cover / present situation: the central issue, current atmosphere, or what is most active around the question.
2. Cross / challenge: the main obstacle, tension, pressure, or support that modifies the present situation.
3. Below / root: underlying cause, motivation, past pattern, or psychological foundation.
4. Behind / recent past: recent events, feelings, conversations, or circumstances that are moving out of focus.
5. Crown / possibility: conscious aim, best available direction, ideal, or potential path.
6. Ahead / near future: the next visible development or situation that may require attention.
7. Self: the user's attitude, feelings, desires, assumptions, or position in the matter.
8. Environment: surrounding people, external factors, relational climate, or how the situation is being influenced from outside.
9. Hopes and fears: what the user wants, fears, expects, or is emotionally bracing for.
10. Possible outcome: the likely direction or synthesis if current patterns continue.

## Interpretation Notes
- Read positions 1 and 2 together first. They often form the core tension of the whole spread.
- Position 10 should not be read alone. Compare it with position 5 (possibility) and position 6 (near future).
- Position 5 can show an available direction or constructive potential, while position 6 shows what is more immediately emerging.
- Position 7 explains the user's inner stance; position 8 shows the outer field or relational environment.
- Use the whole spread to synthesize. Repeated suits, major arcana, court cards, and reversals can show emphasis or imbalance.
- A court card in the outcome position may point to another person's role or to an abstract quality needed for resolution. Do not assume it identifies a specific person unless the question and context support that.

## Caveats
- This spread can become overloaded. Keep the answer organized and prioritize the strongest pattern.
- The possible outcome is a likely direction, not a verdict.
- Do not claim certainty about other people's hidden motives or actions.
- If the spread is mixed, name the tension rather than smoothing it into one simple answer.
- For sensitive topics, keep interpretations symbolic, stabilizing, and grounded.
